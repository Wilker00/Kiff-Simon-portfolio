1st prize, hackathon prototype

Communify. A match, not another feed.

An AI platform for meeting people with overlapping interests. I led the design loop: research, wireframes, prototype. First prize at the Crossingz AI Networking Hackathon.

The work

A short path to a first conversation

Product logic

The profile was a bridge, not the destination.

Communify proposed an editable interest profile built from user-selected and connected data, then used that profile to recommend people and groups with a meaningful overlap.

Explicit user interests

People choose and edit the specific topics used for matchmaking rather than having an algorithm guess in the dark.

Confirm the match

A lightweight swipe interaction lets users accept or skip recommendations before initiating a conversation.

End-to-end journey

Designed onboarding, profile setup, discovery, and messaging so the demo worked as one continuous story during the pitch.

Artifacts

Onboarding, discovery, and the pitch surface.

The additional screens make the rapid design loop visible: establish identity, explain the overlap, then give the user a clear next action.

Recognition

The certificate from the room.

Problem

Most "social" products are feeds with extra steps.

The brief was meaningful connection: matching people who share interests, not another scroll. If the first screen looks like Instagram with a chatbot, the idea is already dead.

Constraints

Hack-week time. Real AI. A hostile demo slot.

We had to integrate matching, not fake it with static cards. The interface had to be interactive enough to demo and simple enough that a judge understood it in seconds.

Decisions

Design decisions under hackathon speed

Feed vs. match

A feed is what social products default to. We led with overlap, why this person, then the conversation. The AI's job is the match, not the chatter.

Prototype vs. pitch deck

Judges can smell slides. Wireframes became a prototype they could watch. The visual presentation had to explain the product without replacing it.

Design with the model in the room

If engineering can't put the matching on that screen, the UX is fiction. Collaboration was the constraint, not a soft skill slide.

Reflection

Winning is not the same as shipping

A 1st-place prototype still has to survive real people who don't care about the hackathon. I'd want a week of matches with strangers who weren't in the room. That's the next test after the Figma file.

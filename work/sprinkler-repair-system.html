Sprinkler Repair System — a wet yard should reach Ben, not a brochure.

A South Florida irrigation company needed a site that gets a call, not a brand story. I designed and built the live site: problem first, phone first, price before work.

The work

Name the leak, then the number.

Problem

People don’t browse sprinkler sites. They have water in the yard.

The customer is standing in a flood, a brown patch, or a bill that jumped. They need to recognize the symptom, trust that a real person shows up, and call. A generic “we’re passionate about irrigation” page wastes that minute.

Decisions

Three forks that made the site a tool

Problem language, not service jargon

The index is “zone won’t shut off,” “timer blank,” “high water bill” — what someone would type or say on the phone — then the repair behind it.

One person, on every screen

Call Ben, text Ben, leave a message. No call center story. Google reviews sit next to the number so the trust is visible before the tap.

Price before work, then the form

The process is four steps: message, onsite diagnosis, quote, repair. Contact is a callback form plus click-to-call, because phone is still fastest in this trade.

Stack

Designed it, then shipped it.

This is client work I own end to end: structure, copy, layout, and the live build. Service area from Riviera Beach to Hollywood, job photos, reviews, FAQ, and a request form that goes to Ben — not a template with the logo swapped.
